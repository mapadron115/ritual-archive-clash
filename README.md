# Ritual Archive: Clash (demo)

An original pixel-art 2D fighting game demo: fifteen original fighters, each with one technique from the Ritual Archive library. No feats, no levels.

## Run
Double-click `index.html`. Nothing to install. (Online play needs an internet connection on both computers.)

## Modes
- 1 Player vs Computer, 2 Players on one keyboard, Watch (computer vs computer)
- Online: one player hosts and gets a 4-letter room code, the other joins with it. The host's game runs the fight.
- The title keeps a silent computer exhibition running behind the bitmap wordmark and four described mode choices. Its label identifies the current fighters and arena. Tab browses the buttons; Enter or Space chooses a mode.
- Choose Archive Courtyard, Rainlit Gallery or Dawn Observatory on the fighter selection screen. Online, the host chooses; rounds and rematches keep that stage. Stages have the same fighting bounds and no hazards.
- Fighter selection shows portraits and two full-body previews, including the second costume in mirror matches. Use Tab to reach a choice, arrow keys to browse its roster, then Enter or Space to select. The Fight button stays below the scrolling choices; full move lists and rules expand beneath the arena choices. Quick controls follow the selected mode.
- Online opponent picks and host arena changes update the existing selection controls, preserving the local player's keyboard focus while both players choose.
- Technique labels use the full card width, wrap at spaces and start below a consistent name/portrait row. On narrow screens, title and selection use the viewport height; fights keep their 16:9 canvas. This remains a keyboard game.
- The title tray uses one compact row on desktop, with its keyboard hint by the logo. Selection cards use smaller portraits and padding at <=900px to give the arena row more room.
- Damage values rise above the struck fighter, with clear space around both fighters, actor tags and banners. Simultaneous values use separate nearby lanes. Hit effects, damage and timing stay the same.
- Round results distinguish K.O., time up and draws, identify the winner and score, and say which round comes next. They remain visible until the round changes. The match screen shows the final score, keeps rematch context clear and tells online guests to wait for the host. Tab, Enter and Space reach the result buttons.
- Escape opens the move list and focuses Resume. Its buttons precede the long rules list; Tab, Enter, Space and scrolling keys keep their native menu behavior. Combat keys stay neutral while the list is open. Offline matches pause; online matches continue, as the list explains. Escape or Resume returns to the fight.
- Online, either player's Change fighters button returns both players to selection. Each keeps control of their own fighter; the host still chooses the arena and starts the next fight.
- While the guest is selecting fighters, delayed fight snapshots are ignored, so old hits and results cannot enter selection. Live updates continue while the online move list is open and resume when the host starts the fight.
- Leaving an online room cancels its pending connection and library callbacks. Delayed events from an old room cannot reopen selection, restart your match or disconnect a replacement room.
- Starting a new fight clears unsent effects from the previous fight, so an old hit cannot arrive with the new match's first online snapshot.
- A gold plaque beneath the round number identifies P1 TIME STOP or P2 TIME STOP while time is stopped, including after the opening flash fades. Rewind does not show it because it does not stop time.
- Attached gum has a broad shaded rose strand and folded knots. It bows when slack and straightens during Slingshot, following both fighters behind their silhouettes. Existing setup, pulls, damage and expiry stay the same.
- Projectile bodies have distinct pixel shapes: pressure bands, curling flame, a hooked wheel, folded fan, brass shot, elastic knot, knife and nail. Active projectile age animates the energy shapes; impact pauses and time stops hold their frames.
- The remaining projectiles use a bevelled rose slash, upright palm pressure sail, low earth crests, faceted stones and a steel shell with a brass collar. Ground and palm waves have distinct silhouettes; the resting imbued stone shares its thrown material. Existing travel, collision and damage stay the same.
- Stage atmosphere uses sixteen discrete phases over 128 ticks: clouds ease through a wind loop, rain continues forward across the reset, and the observatory pointer turns in smaller steps. Stonework and the fighting floor remain still. Stage scenery retains its existing global clock during impact/cinematic pauses.
- Beam attacks have distinct pixel forms: shaded cannon pressure with moving ribs, three blood ribbons, and a fine compressed-air core with paired fins. Existing beam age controls their detail; attack timing and damage remain the same.
- Lingering attraction has inward streams and a faceted dark core that disperses at the burst. Delayed cuts use contracting corner marks followed by crossed blades; the warning leaves the defender's center clear. Existing active ages select these phases.
- The diving Maximum's landing warning has tall bright outer posts, broad gold rails, closing inner arrows and a descending center tip. It marks the locked destination through the existing countdown and clears on impact.
- Rock's charge has an open mint cuff that brightens at each existing strength threshold, with three larger lit indicators visible at native play size. The cue holds during pauses and clears when charging ends, including after interruption.
- Resounding Voice prepares a split jade resonator ahead of the face. Its plates brighten and the compression mark closes through the existing wind-up, clearing when the word releases.
- Convergence gathers three blood feeds toward the forward hand. They close and brighten through its existing wind-up, then clear when the blood beam fires.
- Nuclear Shell has a sustained copper pressure collar ahead of the hands. Its broad face brightens and its vanes close through the existing charge; it clears when the beam fires.
- Web of Strings frames the foe with pink strands that straighten and brighten before each of its three existing hits, then snap and recoil. The center stays open; these strands show the volley rhythm, not a collision boundary.
- Grand Effigy Rite prepares three broad, square-headed nails around the foe, then raises and drops a shaded hammer on the existing final blow. The nail and hammer materials brighten before each hit; the face and body remain clear.
- Standing Ovation's broad gold stones brighten on the next swap's side; paired brackets close before each later clap and recoil afterward. The first hit stays immediate.
- Shrine's four rose cut planes close and brighten around the foe before each existing hit, then split and recoil. They leave the fighter's center clear and show the cut rhythm, not collision boundaries.

- Repeated damage values on the same target use separate rows with a clear gap; each hit keeps its own value and color.

- Art cards show missing nail/gum/wound/history setup, low HP and ground-only restrictions. Active Garden casts show their zero CE cost; an available stone swap shows 0 CE / SWAP STONE. Cards still do not promise that recovery has ended or that a target is in range.

## Rules
- Best of three rounds, 99 seconds each. Punch (three presses chain), kick, jump, guard.
- Arts cost Cursed Energy, which refills by itself and faster when you land blows.
- The Domain (or Maximum) can be used once a round, when its gauge is full or you are under 30% health.
  Two Domains meeting cancel each other.

## Code

`src/sim.js` contains deterministic fight rules; `src/fighters.js` and `src/fighters2.js` define the fifteen techniques. `src/ai.js` controls the computer. `src/pixel.js`, `pixel-stage.js`, `pixel-domain.js`, `pixel-world.js` and `pixel-hud.js` draw original pixel art; `src/render.js` manages impact events; `src/main.js` handles menus, input, synthesized sound and online play. See `ART_DIRECTION.md` for the art rules.
