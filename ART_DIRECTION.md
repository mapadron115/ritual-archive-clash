# Ritual Archive: Clash — original pixel art direction

Owner choice passed on by coordinator 2026-11-14: pixel art for the whole game.

- Source canvas: 640 × 360 for the completed arena/HUD, enlarged with nearest-neighbor sampling. A source pixel represents two simulation units. Final page uses `image-rendering: pixelated`.
- Fighter cells: 144 × 128 source pixels, grounded root (64, 120); standing heights span roughly 85–115 source pixels depending on stance and build. All assets use integer rasterization with fully opaque color clusters and transparent background, never a reduction of the cartoon renderer.
- Silhouettes: dark navy one-pixel outline, thicker by overlap in occluded joints; shadowed far limbs; three colors per fabric/skin/hair, one clear technique accent. Lighting comes from the upper left. Avoid scattered lone highlight pixels, gradients, blur and smoothing.
- Animation: discrete authored anticipation, contact and recovery poses, four walking poses, two idle poses, guard/block, airborne attacks, hit/stun, cast and victory. Gameplay timing chooses the frame; no joint springs or interpolated sprite edges. Feet use the simulation ground, not a new animation collider.
- First fighter: Isolde Varn. Original asymmetrical silver side-sweep, warm face, split navy travel coat, inset ivory shirt, offset cyan clasp, narrow cyan scarf tail and low boots. Her exposed face and angular travel clothes define her own identity.
- Mirror costume: warm pearl coat with mauve shadows, violet trousers and rose accent; silhouette stays the same. Keep P1/P2 overhead tags.
- Roster pass: distinct head shapes, body widths, coat/robe/apron/vest silhouettes, hair, equipment and accents. Shared anatomy is allowed; a palette swap alone does not complete a fighter.
- Stage pass: an original archive courtyard at dusk with architectural depth, stepped cloud clusters, masonry and cloth, a clear foreground fighting plane, and low-contrast distant detail.
- Effects: compact sprite bursts, stepped arcs/rings, bold cores and sparse debris. Preserve Black Flash's dark/red identity and hit-stop without soft glow.
- HUD/menu pass: angular panels, source-pixel borders, bitmap text, consistent native sizing and readable meters.

No existing game's sprites, poses, palettes or stages are used as references or copied assets.

Contact pass (v12): warm directional hit shards, cool cyan guard arcs and wider orange guard-break shards. Two-frame compressed contact, five-frame expansion, then separated fragments; integer mirrored cells keep both directions exact. No soft glow or random frame flicker. Preserve established hit-stop, impact frames and Black Flash identity.

Sound pass (v13): all audio remains original synthesis. A short filtered snap and falling bass body distinguish light/heavy contact; a brighter, shorter pair of tones identifies guard contact. Guard breaks add a falling crack, landings stay quiet, and knockdowns/walls have lower body tones. Black Flash keeps its own dark crack and bass tail. Two-millisecond attacks, shared compression/master gain and capped polyphony control dense effects. Listening on real speakers/headphones remains required.

Silhouette pass (v7): all fifteen idle poses are individually composed. Visible build profiles range from compact/narrow through tall/slender to broad/heavy; shoulders, limbs, stance spread, arm carriage and weight differ. The larger transparent sprite cell leaves room for tall builds, raised fists and the hammer. Simulation hitboxes remain unchanged. Black silhouettes are reviewed separately from color/props.

Animation pass (v9): 49 authored poses per costume, including paired anticipation, attack-specific recoil, settling, launch/fall, curled ground impact, a resting body and two get-up poses. Attack frames follow move time; ground recovery follows the existing down-state timer. Grounded bodies use an exact quarter-turn raster with the lowest opaque pixel on the floor and the body centered at its simulation root. KO bodies stay down. Art contact frames occupy their existing recovery time; no timing, control, damage or collision changes.

Stage expansion (v10): Rainlit Gallery uses cool, recessed canal windows, stone vaults, warm reading lamps, original book bays and slate tiles. Rain is masked behind the window stonework. Dawn Observatory uses an offset copper dome, distant viaduct, brass sky instrument, dawn sky and wind streamers. Each stage has eight cached motion frames; all keep the same clear floor and fighting bounds. The host's stage choice is presentation metadata in the existing match snapshot.

Anticipation refinement (v11): punches pull the shoulder and striking fist visibly behind the root, lower the hips, bend the rear knee and lengthen the front leg. Heavy punches coil lower before rising into contact. Keep these tells inside the existing startup ticks; visual weight transfer never moves the simulation root or collision box.

Airborne/floor pass (v14): three authored poses bridge launch, crest and fast fall. Existing vertical velocity holds the drawing through freezes. Takeoff jets, landing clusters and heavier body-impact dust separate and shrink above the exact floor. Paired land/thud events use one larger cloud; no new combat state or timing.

Projectile pass (v15): short velocity-aligned energy ribbons, blade streaks, stone chips, shell exhaust and gum beads grow after birth and shorten near expiry. Hard-alpha stepped geometry sits behind the original core. Existing projectile age/life/velocity alone drives the drawing, holding through freezes and surviving snapshots without persistent simulation history.

Airborne refinement (v16): arch throws the head back, opens the arms and separates the legs; crest folds the knees up against the chest; fall turns the torso forward and reaches both hands toward the floor. Face, hair and accessories tilt together with integer-raster geometry. Review the three phases as flat silhouettes across the whole roster. Existing velocity thresholds, physics and root positions remain unchanged.

Summon refinement (v17): hounds use four authored bounds with planted, gathered and suspended paws, shaded far legs, separate jaw contact/release and a still waiting stance. Nue has four distinct wing silhouettes, velocity-selected dive/bank angles and swept wings after contact. Toad crouches, stretches and recoils around its existing tongue lifetime. Finite cached integer cells mirror exactly; no sprite resampling or new simulation fields. Keep the original spell timing, damage, summon movement and collision rules.

Summon materials (v18): match the fighters' navy contour and three-tone volumes. Hounds have broad lit shoulders, shaded bellies, joint lines and individually outlined paws separated under the haunch and shoulder in tucked bounds. Nue uses dark trailing feathers, warm middle planes and inset leading-edge highlights; Toad has a shaded belly, lit back and outlined forelegs/toes. Keep highlight clusters inside the silhouette so thin bright strokes do not erase the dark edge.

Art-family motion (v19): slash uses a high rearward load, downward sweep and low follow-through; cannon braces two forearms low around the established muzzle height and rocks backward after firing. Claps bring separated hands together forward, then separate; signs open below the chest; bow draws, releases the rear hand and lowers the front arm; throws swing past the release. Twenty added poses bring each build/costume to 72. Release hands sit near the existing projectile/beam origins. Startup, contact and recovery still use only the original move clocks, including short recoveries and hit-stop. Judge contact/recoil/settle as flat silhouettes, not only colored frames.

Cast-family motion (v20): generic casts draw back, open a vertical palm at the existing projectile height, recoil down and return toward guard. Spoken arts inhale with the head tilted back, lean forward with an open mouth for the word, then brace the throat and straighten. Only the three existing speech-art IDs route to this presentation family; their combat definitions stay untouched. Seven new poses bring each build/costume to 79. Use existing move clocks, exact mirrors, navy contours and three-tone materials; preserve immediate state transitions such as Blast Dash, even when they skip a release/recovery drawing.

Domain/Maximum motion (v21): the existing cinematic countdown gathers the arms, closes a high seal and opens a wide Domain silhouette. Maximums coil low, draw back and prepare their own release. Beams brace and recoil at their real firing ticks; voice, repeated claps, hammer throws, slashes and string pulls use their existing effect rhythms. The diving Maximum has separate lifted knees, descending fist and low impact poses. Eleven new original poses bring each build/costume to 90. The first Domain effect already happens before the cinematic freeze in the simulation; the drawings never delay it. After that introduction, only Domain time or the original cast countdown selects frames. Preserve interruptions, instant movement, buffs, freedom to attack after unheld casts, and all collision/timing rules.
